## 6. Expected Outcomes and Contributions

Building on the analytical objective defined in Section 3, the project is expected to produce an integrated and reproducible framework for examining and forecasting changes in the slope of the Canadian yield curve. The first expected outcome is a documented data pipeline that extracts, aligns, cleans, and transforms macro-financial time series from the Bank of Canada, the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis, and Statistics Canada. This pipeline will generate a consistent analytical dataset while preserving data provenance and supporting transparent replication.

The project’s academic contribution will be empirical evidence on the temporal relationships among monetary-policy rates, sovereign bond yields, inflation, and the USD/CAD exchange rate. The analysis will assess whether these variables provide meaningful explanatory or forecasting information for movements in the 10-year minus 2-year Canadian yield spread.

Its methodological contribution will be a controlled comparison of ARIMA, VAR, LSTM, and naïve random-walk forecasts using common prediction horizons, chronological validation procedures, and consistent evaluation metrics. This comparison will help determine the trade-off between forecasting accuracy, interpretability, stability, and model complexity rather than assuming that the most advanced method will perform best.

The practical contribution will be an executive Power BI dashboard that presents the yield-curve slope, Canada–U.S. yield differentials, rolling volatility, policy-rate changes, and model forecasts. Together, the dataset, reproducible code, model evaluation, and dashboard will provide an academic decision-support resource for analysts examining Canadian fixed-income markets. Its recommendations will remain conditional on the observed evidence and the documented limitations of the data and models.
The main practical contribution will be an executive Power BI dashboard that communicates yield-curve conditions, model outputs, 
and relevant risk indicators in an accessible format. Collectively, the dataset, reproducible code, model evaluation, and visual 
reporting will provide a transparent academic decision-support framework for analysts studying Canadian fixed-income markets, 
while also documenting limitations that must be considered before any real-world financial application.
