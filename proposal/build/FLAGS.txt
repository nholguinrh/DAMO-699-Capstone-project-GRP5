ISSUE 1 — ARIMA is promised in three sections but absent from the methodology.

Sections 03, 06, and 07 all commit the project to an ARIMA model. Section 05, the
section that actually specifies the analytical approach, never mentions it. Section
05 specifies only VAR, LSTM, and a Random Walk (naive) benchmark.

Where each section says it:

  03_analytical_objective.md   "The study will compare ARIMA as a univariate
                                baseline, VAR ... and LSTM"
  06_expected_outcomes.md      "a controlled comparison of ARIMA, VAR, LSTM, and
                                naive random-walk forecasts"
  07_project_plan.md           "the team will implement ARIMA, VAR, and LSTM models"
                                (Weeks 6-8)
  05_analytical_approach.md    ARIMA does not appear. Section 5.3 covers VAR and
                                LSTM. Section 5.4 names Random Walk as the baseline.

Why it matters: this is exactly what the holistic rubric pass in NOTES.md check 2
looks for -- "does section 3's RQ match section 5's method match section 7's plan?"
-- and cross-section coherence is graded under "Project Organization and
Professional Writing." A reader following the document in order is told the
univariate baseline is ARIMA (03), then handed a methodology where the baseline is
a Random Walk (05), then told ARIMA gets implemented in Weeks 6-8 (07).

Two ways to resolve; pick one, do not do both:

  (a) Add ARIMA to section 05 -- give it a subsection under 5.3 alongside VAR and
      LSTM, and state how it relates to the Random Walk baseline in 5.4 (ARIMA as
      the univariate statistical baseline, Random Walk as the naive floor). This
      keeps 03/06/07 as written and adds words to 05.

  (b) Drop ARIMA from 03, 06, and 07 -- let the Random Walk stand as the sole
      baseline, matching 05 as currently written. This removes words from three
      sections.

Note the interaction with the page limit: section 05 is already 953 words against a
375-word target and is one of the two sections driving the proposal over the 5-page
cap. Option (a) makes the overage worse. Option (b) reduces it slightly. That is a
reason to weigh (b), but it is Lerneir's and the owners' call, not a formatting
decision -- do not pick (b) purely to save space if ARIMA genuinely belongs in the
model comparison.


ISSUE 2 — VECM appears only in section 05 (lower severity, same root cause).

Section 05 (5.2) raises a Vector Error Correction Model as a possible extension if
the Johansen test finds cointegration. No other section mentions VECM -- not the
objective (03), not the expected outcomes (06), and not the project plan (07), so
no timeline slot exists for building it.

This is defensible as written, because 05 frames VECM as conditional ("may be
warranted") rather than promised, and the section explicitly says it "does not
commit to a final model selection." Flagging it only so the decision is deliberate:
if VECM is a real possibility, 07 should leave room for it in Weeks 6-8; if it is
not, consider whether the conditional mention earns its words given the page
pressure on 05.
